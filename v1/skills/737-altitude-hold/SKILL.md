# Boeing 737 altitude capture and hold

- Read actual altitude, vertical speed, IAS, FMA, and selected altitude. Verify the altimeter setting and assigned datum; do not select standard pressure solely because the aircraft is leveling off.
- Anticipate level-off rather than waiting to cross the target. Within about 1000 ft of the assigned altitude, reduce a high manual climb rate toward 1000 ft/min or less while preserving safe IAS. A usual final capture lead is approximately 10% of the climb rate in feet: about 150 ft at 1500 ft/min. Also calculate seconds to the target as remaining feet divided by vertical speed in ft/s; allow time for the complete reading/decision/control cycle and for the aircraft to reduce its climb rate. Start earlier if the usual lead is insufficient; a changed pitch ratio without falling vertical speed is not capture.
- With automation, verify CMD engagement, the expected altitude-acquisition and then altitude-hold FMA, the selected target, and the actual reduction of vertical speed. ALT HOLD normally holds the altitude at engagement; pressing it above the assigned altitude does not command a return to the MCP target. If capture fails, take manual control and level off; do not assume the selected altitude is a hard limit without a functioning capture mode.
- During manual level-off, ease toward level-flight attitude and adjust thrust to maintain the safe planned speed. With autothrottle inactive, manage thrust yourself rather than retaining takeoff thrust through capture. Hold the needed elevator input while stabilizing, then trim to relieve pressure with verified small changes. Make small corrections based on both altitude error and vertical-speed trend; avoid alternating large pitch commands around the target.
- If the assigned altitude is overshot, first arrest the climb at a safe attitude and speed, then establish a controlled return to the assigned altitude and recapture with an appropriate lead. Do not continue climbing while diagnosing mode buttons or finish merely because the first capture was missed.
- Confirm sustained level flight inside the assigned tolerance, with near-zero vertical speed and stable IAS. If no tighter tolerance is specified, aim to stay within 100 ft. A single crossing of the target is not a hold.
- Keep reading instruments and correcting drift until the scenario stops or new authority changes the objective. Do not continue toward a higher FMC cruise altitude or proceed to the route's descent phase when the task ends at an intermediate altitude; a controlled correction of an altitude overshoot is recovery, not a new phase objective. Finish the applicable checklist checks only with actual supporting evidence.
