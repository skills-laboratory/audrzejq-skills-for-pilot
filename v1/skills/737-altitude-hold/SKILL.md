# Boeing 737 altitude capture and hold

- Read actual altitude, vertical speed, IAS, FMA, and selected altitude. Verify the altimeter setting and assigned datum; do not select standard pressure solely because the aircraft is leveling off.
- Anticipate level-off rather than waiting to cross the target. A usual manual lead is approximately 10% of climb rate in feet: about 150 ft at 1500 ft/min. Also estimate seconds to the target as altitude remaining divided by vertical speed in ft/s. If this is no longer than the measured read–decision–control interval plus time to arrest the climb, suspend cleanup/checklist work and begin capture now; the usual lead alone is insufficient with slow feedback or excessive climb rate.
- With automation, verify the expected altitude-acquisition and then altitude-hold FMA, the selected target, and the actual reduction of vertical speed. If capture fails, take manual control and level off; do not assume the selected altitude is a hard limit without a functioning capture mode.
- During manual level-off, use small pitch corrections followed immediately by complete flight-instrument scans. Release nose-down input before the projected attitude reaches level-flight attitude, then counter any continuing pitch drift; do not leave a capture correction held for flap or checklist calls. Adjust thrust to maintain the safe planned speed, and trim once stable. Use altitude error together with pitch rate and vertical-speed trend, not altitude crossings alone, to avoid repeated climb/descent excursions.
- Confirm sustained level flight inside the assigned tolerance, with near-zero vertical speed and stable IAS. If no tighter tolerance is specified, aim to stay within 100 ft. A single crossing of the target is not a hold.
- Keep reading instruments and correcting drift until the scenario stops or new authority changes the objective. Do not continue toward a higher FMC cruise altitude or begin descent when the task ends at an intermediate altitude. Finish the applicable checklist checks only with actual supporting evidence.
