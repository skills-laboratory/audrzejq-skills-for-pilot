# Boeing 737 altitude capture and hold

- Read actual altitude, vertical speed, IAS, FMA, and selected altitude. Verify the altimeter setting and assigned datum; do not select standard pressure solely because the aircraft is leveling off.
- Anticipate level-off rather than waiting to cross the target. For manual capture, a usual lead is approximately 10% of the climb rate in feet: about 150 ft at 1500 ft/min. Start reducing pitch earlier when the climb rate or tool latency is high, and monitor the response continuously.
- With automation, verify the expected altitude-acquisition and then altitude-hold FMA, the selected target, and the actual reduction of vertical speed. ALT HLD can hold the current altitude rather than climb to the selected one; do not select it early as a substitute for acquisition. If capture fails, verify autopilot disengagement and level off manually; do not assume the selected altitude is a hard limit without a functioning capture mode.
- During manual level-off, ease toward level-flight attitude, adjust thrust to maintain the safe planned speed, and trim once stable. Make small corrections based on both altitude error and vertical-speed trend; avoid alternating large pitch commands around the target.
- Confirm sustained level flight inside the assigned tolerance, with near-zero vertical speed and stable IAS. If no tighter tolerance is specified, aim to stay within 100 ft. A single crossing of the target is not a hold. If stable level flight is below the target, establish only a modest, monitored climb and taper it as the target approaches; remove the nose-up correction and arrest the pitch trend rather than leaving it held to cover the remaining altitude. Recover attitude and safe speed before correcting a large altitude error.
- Keep reading instruments and correcting drift until the scenario stops or new authority changes the objective. Do not continue toward a higher FMC cruise altitude or begin descent when the task ends at an intermediate altitude. Finish the applicable checklist checks only with actual supporting evidence.
