# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes. Brief the rejected-takeoff criteria and supported braking controls, including a failed thrust/mode response; do not search for these during the roll.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Distinguish suggested values from entered/confirmed values and confirm the required entries. Keep V1 and VR separately labeled: V1 is the decision speed, not the rotation speed. Do not rotate from a predicted speed without a fresh IAS read confirming VR.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration; arming does not establish an active mode.

## Roll and rotate

1. For a stationary runway start, hold the aircraft with appropriate brakes through preparation and engine stabilization at approximately 40% N1 on both engines. Use measured N1 rather than thrust-lever percentage; do not consume runway calibrating the lever or marking preparation items. Release the brakes and push TO/GA in the prescribed sequence, then promptly read both engines, live A/T ARM, and FMA. Required takeoff thrust is the FMC/performance N1 target, not merely N1 above 40% or increasing IAS. If TO/GA has no response, use only a documented manual-thrust branch and verify both engines reach the required target; manual thrust does not establish TO/GA modes. If required thrust/modes cannot be established promptly before V1, reject using the prepared procedure rather than continue toward VR while troubleshooting.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches.
3. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. Read pitch, IAS, vertical speed, and height AGL immediately after the command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Use successive fresh pitch readings to determine the current rate; a longer-window trend can still show a rise after pitch has started falling. Around 10°, progressively ease rotation input only while the latest rate shows continued rise toward the target. Include reading, decision, and control-call delay when estimating time to the target. Neutral is not an attitude lock, and a large reduction in back-pressure can allow the nose to fall: adjust in small steps with immediate read-backs to arrest either trend while preserving safe IAS. If pitch still rises toward an overshoot, use a brief, small nose-down correction and read immediately. Do not base a reduction on an old near-target attitude or a positive vertical speed alone.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Confirm airborne state and positive climb from rising altitude and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.

## If rejecting

- When a reject is warranted before V1, promptly close thrust levers, deploy speedbrakes, apply maximum appropriate braking, and use reverse thrust as prescribed through supported controls. Verify thrust reduction and ground-speed deceleration; do not leave thrust applied while reporting the failure. Use the parking brake only after stopping, not as a substitute for service braking during the roll.
- At or beyond V1, a mode anomaly alone is not a reason to reject: follow the applicable failure procedure and continue if the aircraft can fly safely. Do not improvise a high-speed reject, an early autopilot engagement, or a departure from off-runway ground to compensate for a tool problem.
