# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule for an airborne search.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Distinguish suggested values from entered/confirmed values. Do not use a remembered fixed rotation speed.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration; arming does not establish an active mode. Do not release the brakes with unresolved performance, target, or mode prerequisites.

## Roll and rotate

1. With brakes held, adjust thrust until measured N1 is approximately 40% and stabilized on both engines; setting the thrust levers to 40% is not this check. Push TO/GA once as prescribed; read engine and FMA indications to confirm the intended takeoff thrust/modes before beginning the roll. If TO/GA is unverified and N1/FMA do not show the expected outcome, resolve the documented prerequisites while stopped. Do not substitute a guessed reading named after the button or a checklist mark for that outcome.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches.
3. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. If already airborne near that attitude with pitch rising, arrest the trend rather than starting another nose-up rotation input. Read flight instruments immediately after the pitch command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Around 10°, move the rotation input toward neutral rather than merely halving a positive ratio and leaving it held. Estimate time remaining to the target as remaining degrees divided by observed pitch rate; include the observed full interval between instrument reads, not just tool execution time, before the next correction. If that delay consumes the remaining time, remove the rotation input now. Neutral is not an attitude lock: if pitch still rises toward an overshoot, use a brief, small nose-down correction and read the response immediately. Stabilize actual pitch and its trend, not the yoke ratio.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Confirm airborne state and positive climb from rising altitude and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.
