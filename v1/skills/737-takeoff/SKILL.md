# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, rejected-takeoff procedure, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule or reject criteria for a moving-aircraft search.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Distinguish suggested values from entered/confirmed values. Do not use a remembered fixed rotation speed.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration; arming does not establish an active mode.

## Roll and rotate

1. Use the documented brake controls to remain stopped during engine stabilization. Advance thrust to approximately 40% N1 on both engines, using measured N1 rather than thrust-lever percentage. A throttle value sets a sustained lever position, not an N1 target: make a small adjustment and read both engines' N1 and trends as they spool before changing it again. Verify both engines have stabilized; do not chase the delayed response with repeated large lever changes while consuming runway.
2. Once stabilized, proceed to TO/GA and brake release in the prescribed sequence without further setup work. Approximately 40% N1 is only the stabilization stage, not the thrust to maintain until VR. Push TO/GA once as prescribed and immediately verify the intended FMA modes and both engines approaching the configured FMC takeoff N1. An ARM annunciation alone does not establish takeoff thrust. If thrust or modes fail to establish, remain stopped or reject under the applicable procedure; do not troubleshoot by repeated TO/GA presses or blindly selecting maximum lever position during the roll.
3. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, engine symmetry, and remaining runway. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches. Inadequate acceleration or missing takeoff thrust calls for the rejected-takeoff decision, not continued stabilization-thrust adjustments while waiting for VR.
4. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. Read flight instruments immediately after the pitch command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Around 10°, move the rotation input toward neutral rather than merely halving a positive ratio and leaving it held. Estimate time remaining to the target as remaining degrees divided by observed pitch rate; include reading, decision, and control-call delay before the next correction. If that delay consumes the remaining time, remove the rotation input now. Neutral is not an attitude lock: if pitch still rises toward an overshoot, use a brief, small nose-down correction and read the response immediately. Stabilize actual pitch and its trend, not the yoke ratio.
5. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
6. Confirm airborne state and positive climb from rising altitude and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.

## Interrupted takeoff

- Before V1, reject promptly for failure to establish planned thrust or inadequate acceleration under the applicable rejected-takeoff criteria. Close thrust, maintain directional control, and use the prescribed braking and reverse-thrust procedure, verifying deceleration. At or above V1, use the approved continuation/rejection criteria rather than improvising a late reject.
- Keep monitoring until successive ground-speed readings confirm the aircraft is stopped before setting the parking brake or claiming a stop. After a runway excursion or hard impact, do not reapply takeoff thrust to diagnose controls or attempt another takeoff; secure the aircraft as prescribed and report its actual condition and location.
