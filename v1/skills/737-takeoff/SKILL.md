# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule for an airborne search.
- Before brake release, read `takeoff-data` and `v-speeds` or their documented equivalents for V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim at the actual weight and conditions. Verify entered speeds and actual flap/trim match the configured performance data; selected MCP speed alone does not establish VR or V2. Resolve missing or unconfirmed data while stopped rather than using remembered speeds.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration; arming does not establish an active mode.

## Roll and rotate

1. Keep the aircraft stopped during preparatory checks and stabilization at approximately 40% N1 on both engines; use measured N1, not thrust-lever percentage. Initiate the roll and push TO/GA as prescribed, then immediately verify the planned takeoff thrust and FMA. If these are absent, resolve prerequisites or the documented manual-thrust branch while stopped, or apply the documented rejected-takeoff criteria if already rolling. Do not continue an extended roll at stabilization thrust or treat `ok` as proof TO/GA worked.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches.
3. At confirmed VR, rotate toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second and immediately read pitch, pitch rate, and IAS. Around 10°, select neutral rather than leaving a reduced positive ratio held; do this earlier if the measured full read-to-control delay would carry pitch to 15° before the next correction. Make this easing command before another read or secondary task. Neutral does not lock attitude: if pitch still rises toward an overshoot, use a small nose-down correction and immediately re-read. Stabilize pitch and its trend, not the yoke ratio.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Confirm airborne state, rising altitude, and positive vertical speed, arrest excess pitch rise, then select gear UP; this is a positive-climb action, not a task to defer until altitude capture. Read pitch and IAS immediately afterward and interleave flight-instrument reads with handle and all-gear/transit indications until retraction is complete. An unverified gear call requires these actual-state checks; do not reverse a selection merely because its immediate text is unchanged. Report contradictory feedback without claiming an unverified condition. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.
