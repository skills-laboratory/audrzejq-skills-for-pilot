# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule for an airborne search.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Distinguish suggested values from entered/confirmed values. Do not use a remembered fixed rotation speed.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish and read back the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration before TO/GA; blank FMA with flight director OFF or autothrottle unarmed is not a prepared autoflight system. Arming does not establish an active mode.

## Roll and rotate

1. Keep brakes set while bringing both engines to approximately 40% measured N1 and verifying stabilization. Adjust thrust-lever travel from the N1 feedback; a 40% lever command does not mean 40% N1 and cannot verify that checklist item. Release brakes and push TO/GA in the documented sequence; read engine and FMA indications to confirm the intended takeoff thrust/modes. Resolve an unverified TO/GA response before assuming takeoff thrust is established, rather than repeatedly pressing switches or guessing a lever percentage.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches.
3. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. Read flight instruments immediately after the pitch command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Do not increase back-pressure when the existing pitch trend will reach the target before another correction can take effect. Around 10°, move the rotation input toward neutral rather than merely halving a positive ratio and leaving it held. Estimate time remaining as remaining degrees divided by observed pitch rate, including reading, decision, and control-call delay. If that delay consumes the remaining time, remove the rotation input now. At or above 15° with rising pitch, stop the rotation input and arrest the rise before a gear or checklist call. Neutral is not an attitude lock: use a brief, small nose-down correction if needed and read the response immediately. Stabilize actual pitch and its trend, not the yoke ratio.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Confirm airborne state and positive climb from rising altitude and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.
