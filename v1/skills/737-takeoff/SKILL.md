# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Load `737-checklist` and `737-climb` while stopped, then read the applicable takeoff procedure and live checklist. A procedure document does not replace checklist discovery or recording. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule for an airborne search.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Resolve unset V-speeds, missing performance inputs, or a trim mismatch through the documented setup, then read back confirmed speeds and configuration. Calculated suggestions alone are not a completed takeoff setup. Do not use a remembered fixed rotation speed.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Establish the prescribed flight-director and autothrottle configuration; arming does not establish an active mode.

## Roll and rotate

1. Adjust thrust until measured N1 stabilizes at approximately 40% on both engines; entering 40% throttle does not request 40% N1. Push TO/GA once as prescribed, then verify both engines reach the performance takeoff N1 target and the expected FMA appears. If TO/GA is unverified or thrust/modes fail to change, resolve the documented flight-director/autothrottle prerequisites and recheck. If the procedure permits manual takeoff thrust, set and verify that N1 target manually; otherwise remain stopped and report the blocker. Acceleration alone does not prove takeoff thrust, and stabilization thrust is not the takeoff setting.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Near VR, use focused instrument reads rather than documentation searches.
3. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. Read pitch and IAS together immediately after the pitch command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Around 10°, ease toward the documented neutral input rather than leaving the rotation ratio held. Estimate time to the target from remaining degrees and observed pitch rate, including reading, decision, and control-call delay; ease now if another cycle would reach it. Reaching 15° with pitch still rising is not stabilization. Use brief, small counter-corrections with immediate reads to arrest the trend before gear or checklist work; neutral is not an attitude lock.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Confirm airborne state and positive climb from rising altitude and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.
