# Boeing 737 takeoff

## Before advancing thrust

- Read the applicable takeoff procedure, live checklist, and `737-climb` while stopped. Establish the departure track, assigned altitude, initial climb speed, acceleration conditions, actual flap-retraction detents/speeds, and autoflight plan before releasing the brakes; do not leave the retraction schedule for an airborne search.
- Discover and read the tool schemas for thrust, brakes, pitch/roll/rudder, MCP, configuration changes, and focused instrument reads while stopped. For manual control, verify joystick override ON and neutral pitch/roll/rudder setpoints before movement; check the state before using a toggle. Prepare the read/rotate/read sequence now, not after reaching VR.
- Obtain V1, VR, V2, takeoff thrust, flap detent, and trim from the configured FMC/performance data for the actual weight and conditions. Distinguish calculated suggestions from entered/confirmed values. Zero/unset V-speeds, invalid calculated trim, or a trim mismatch are unresolved preparation, not permission to roll: use supported FMC/performance controls to resolve them and read back the result. Do not invent values or use a remembered fixed rotation speed; if preparation cannot be completed, remain stopped and report the blocker.
- Verify flap configuration, gear down, speedbrake down, trim, brakes, required lights, transponder, and runway/takeoff authority. Set and read back the assigned MCP altitude and the procedure's takeoff speed/heading. Verify flight directors ON and autothrottle armed when prescribed; arming does not establish an active mode. Keep CMD disengaged for the takeoff roll and hand-fly rotation; autopilot engagement is not a way to obtain liftoff.
- Keep brakes held until the preparation checks above are verified and applicable ground checklist items are recorded. A loaded route and running engines do not establish takeoff readiness.

## Roll and rotate

1. With brakes holding the aircraft stopped, adjust thrust until measured N1 is approximately 40% on both engines and stabilized; a 40% thrust-lever command is not 40% N1. Record that observed condition before committing to the roll. Release brakes and push TO/GA once as prescribed, then read engine and FMA indications to confirm the intended takeoff thrust/modes. An unverified press, blank FMA, or N1 still at stabilization thrust is not a successful takeoff-thrust selection. If takeoff thrust fails to establish before V1, reject using the applicable rejected-takeoff procedure rather than diagnose while accelerating; at or above V1 use the applicable continuation/emergency procedure.
2. Maintain centerline with small directional corrections and monitor IAS, acceleration, and engine symmetry. Estimate time to VR from the remaining speed increment and observed IAS trend, including the full reading/decision/control delay. Use the prepared focused read/control sequence without deliberate waits, narration, or tool-discovery round trips near VR: these delays consume runway even while no cockpit command is being sent.
3. At the confirmed VR, rotate smoothly toward approximately 15° at about 2–3° per second. Read flight instruments immediately after the pitch command, aiming for 1–2 second feedback without deliberate waits. Around 10°, move the rotation input toward neutral rather than merely halving a positive ratio and leaving it held. Estimate time remaining to the target as remaining degrees divided by observed pitch rate; include reading, decision, and control-call delay before the next correction. If that delay consumes the remaining time, remove the rotation input now. Neutral is not an attitude lock: if pitch still rises toward an overshoot, use a brief, small nose-down correction and read the response immediately. Stabilize actual pitch and its trend, not the yoke ratio.
4. After liftoff, follow valid flight-director guidance and the procedure's initial climb speed, normally V2 plus its specified margin. If pitch continues above the target or IAS falls toward V2, reduce back-pressure immediately and check the response; do not hold the rotation input to obtain a larger climb rate.
5. Require on-ground FALSE, safe airborne IAS, and successive readings showing rising altitude/height AGL and positive vertical speed before selecting gear UP. A climb phase label, one positive AGL/vertical-speed reading, or ground bouncing is not positive climb. Before the gear call, ease rotation input as needed from the latest pitch trend; do not leave back-pressure unchanged while attending to the gear. Read pitch and IAS immediately after the selection, and interleave flight-instrument reads with gear-transit verification through completion. Continue with `737-climb`.

Record each verified checklist action using `737-checklist`. Do not let marking, gear reads, or roll-mode selection interrupt pitch and IAS control.
