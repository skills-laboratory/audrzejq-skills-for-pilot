# Boeing 737 approach preparation and ILS intercept

## Prepare while the aircraft remains controlled

1. Load `737-checklist`, `737-landing`, and `737-go-around`. Read the current descent/approach sections, selected chart, complete aircraft ILS procedure including its capture continuation and autoland/rollout reference, and missed approach while stable automation maintains a safe flight path. Brief the authorized landing control plan and its channel/flare checks before intercept; the approach-arming page alone does not cover touchdown. Do not disengage functioning CMD simply to begin preparation.
2. Read actual position, IAS/groundspeed, altitude/QNH, configuration, FMA, and selected targets. Establish the published inbound course, entry/intercept altitude, distance reference, runway threshold elevation, minimums, and missed-approach track/altitude. Distinguish chart DME, ILS DME, and distance to threshold; do not substitute one for another.
3. Budget preparation to the planned glide-slope intercept, not touchdown: minutes available are distance to that intercept in NM divided by groundspeed in kt, multiplied by 60. Recheck distance, beam movement, and FMA between setup blocks; ALT HOLD on an inbound heading does not preserve the intercept opportunity. Have landing performance, minimums, radios, and applicable preparation checks verified before the beam reaches capture. If any remains unresolved as capture approaches, stop CDU/document troubleshooting and use the published missed approach or an authorized safe reposition; do not start final descent with incomplete setup.
4. Verify landing altitude/pressurization, system recall, local altimeter setting, minimums, required lights/signs, navigation performance as applicable, and autobrake. For BARO minimums, set the charted decision altitude AMSL, not the parenthesized height above threshold; use radio decision height only when the selected procedure permits it. Read back both minimums type and value before marking the item. Resolve landing performance as below before final descent.
5. Tune both receivers to the charted ILS frequency and verify both identifiers and courses. Use supported reading identifiers from the current catalog. Select speeds compatible with the current flap configuration and its maneuver/extension limits; do not command final landing speed while still clean. Configure progressively and read actual flap/slat movement after each selection.

## Resolve landing performance without guessing

- Read current gross weight and fuel with their units, then establish the CDU's weight units and entry scale. CDU weights may be tonnes or thousands of pounds: kg / 1,000 gives tonnes; kg × 2.2046 / 1,000 gives thousands of pounds. Cross-check displayed CDU fuel against measured fuel in the same units; do not assume the CDU uses kg because an instrument does. If required by the documented initialization procedure, ZFW is gross weight minus fuel.
- Follow displayed page labels and supported line-select actions. A short `clr` can dismiss an error while restoring the rejected text; subsequent presses can delete only one character each. Read `fmc-cdu.scratchpad` after dismissing a message and clear the underlying text with supported actions until the read-back is empty. Only then type the correctly scaled weight, require the scratchpad to match that entry exactly before line selection, and verify the accepted field and calculated VREF afterward. Do not append new text to an old entry. INVALID ENTRY calls for checking page, field, units, scale, and format, not trying unrelated units or fields. An out-of-date navigation database message alone does not establish failed weight/VREF calculation or unusable radio ILS.
- Obtain landing flaps and weight-dependent VREF from accepted APPROACH REF data or an available approved aircraft performance source, then apply the required speed correction. Placeholders and scratchpad text are not accepted data. If a corrected, documented attempt still fails and no approved source is available, report the unresolved limitation and stabilize/reposition at safe altitude; do not invent VREF or descend while troubleshooting.

## Preserve the published approach geometry

- Verify that the aircraft is on the approach side of the intended threshold and will fly the published inbound course toward it. A bearing to the airport from the opposite side is not the inbound ILS course. Do not replace the published course with its reciprocal or attempt to use the same ILS for the opposite runway.
- Use charted fixes or verified runway coordinates for repositioning; blank or all-zero runway metadata is unavailable, not a runway location. Coordinate bearings are true: convert the magnetic course using charted variation (add easterly, subtract westerly, wrap at 360 degrees) before comparison. Do not infer a localizer origin or DME offset from guessed beacon coordinates. If navigation evidence conflicts with the chart, maintain safe altitude and resolve it before descent.
- Check motion using timestamped position samples or the reported trend window, not the number of reads. One degree of latitude is 60 NM; northward speed in kt is latitude change per second × 60 × 3,600. Longitude needs the cosine-of-latitude correction. Compare with groundspeed, not IAS. Increasing DME after passing abeam/beyond the field is expected; lost identification outside reception coverage does not prove earlier inbound guidance was faulty. Report observations separately from unconfirmed simulator-fault explanations.
- A centered or zero localizer value with a missing identifier is not evidence of alignment. Require valid identification/signal indications, correct runway-side geometry, and appropriate active FMA before relying on guidance.

## Cross-check the glidepath before changing altitude

- Use the selected chart's altitude-versus-DME table first. An independent 3-degree check is threshold elevation + reference datum height + about 318 ft per NM to threshold. This is AMSL altitude, not height AGL; use the charted DME-to-threshold relationship, not an assumed offset. The estimate never authorizes descent below a published restriction.
- Compare actual barometric altitude with the path at the current distance. Below path means maintain the safe charted intercept altitude and let the descending beam approach as distance closes; descending faster moves farther below it. Descend toward an intercept altitude only when chart and clearance permit. Once high and too close for normal below-beam capture, reposition/go around immediately: do not lower the MCP floor toward minimums and escalate V/S to rescue the approach. Active VOR/LOC with G/S merely armed is lateral guidance, not permission for a catch-up descent.
- Establish deviation sign and flag meanings from the installed receiver's documentation and corroborate with the chart calculation. A pegged ±2.5-dot indication can mean an out-of-scale beam; zero trend while pegged is not by itself a frozen receiver, nor is it glide-slope alive/capture. Check identification, validity flags, distance, and altitude before declaring a fault; do not lower the altitude target to test suspect guidance.

## Intercept from below and verify each transition

1. Establish a safe charted intercept altitude below the glidepath and a suitable heading, preferably within 30 degrees of the inbound course. Preserve altitude restrictions; do not dive from above close to the runway to chase the glide slope.
2. If a chart-authorized descent to intercept is needed, select its altitude floor and activate V/S, then read the FMA and set/read back the intended descent target in supported 100 ft/min increments. Mode activation can synchronize V/S; a setter reporting zero or another value does not establish the requested descent. Check actual altitude, IAS, and vertical-speed response before another adjustment. ALT/ACQ followed by ALT HOLD near the target is normal capture, not a reason to toggle modes or lower the floor.
3. With valid localizer identification, safe geometry, and an established intercept, arm APP once and verify VOR/LOC and G/S armed separately; do not wait for the glide-slope needle to leave its stop before arming. Localizer capture requires active VOR/LOC with valid, converging lateral guidance; APP illumination or heading equal to inbound course is insufficient. If lateral deviation grows while HDG SEL remains active, correct the intercept heading within safe geometry and recheck rather than continue unchanged. Glide slope alive requires valid vertical guidance moving toward capture, not a pegged value or a single zero reading.
4. At the procedure's glide-slope-alive condition, select gear DOWN and verify nose/left/right safe indications, flaps 15 with actual configuration, both engine start switches CONT, and speedbrake ARM with its distinct ARMED annunciator. Use documented detents, not an assumed percentage, and record verified applicable items with live checklist IDs.
5. Glide-slope capture requires active G/S, valid guidance, and a steady descending flight path. At capture, select the planned landing flaps within their limits, verify configuration and corrected VREF target, then set/read back the charted missed-approach altitude. Immediately recheck that G/S remains active: loss of G/S with a higher selected altitude can command a climb.
6. At the first verified G/S capture, execute the landing-control checks in `737-landing` before secondary checklist recording or waiting for descent. Tracking VOR/LOC and G/S does not establish a flare-capable landing. If valid capture or a stable landing cannot be achieved, use `737-go-around`; do not repeatedly toggle APP or repair a late approach with heading/V/S/level-change modes.
