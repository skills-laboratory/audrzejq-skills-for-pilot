# Boeing 737 ILS approach

## Intercept and capture

1. Complete `737-descent` preparation and read the live approach and landing sections. Verify the runway, charted altitudes and minimums, landing speed, identified receivers and both selected inbound courses. Use current position and track to confirm you are approaching the correct end of the runway. MCP headings and chart courses are magnetic unless stated otherwise; do not confuse a geographic true bearing with a magnetic heading.
2. Establish a suitable localizer intercept, preferably within 30° of the inbound course, at the chart's safe intercept altitude with the glide slope above the aircraft. Use HDG SEL only for the intercept, with position and signal feedback. Do not keep turning toward the airport while descending below the safe intercept altitude.
3. Arm APP once and read the FMA: VOR/LOC and G/S armed are not capture. Require active VOR/LOC for lateral capture and active G/S for vertical capture, together with valid identified signals and plausible deviations. Active G/S with a full-scale pointer or a flight path inconsistent with the chart is not safe captured guidance; discontinue the approach rather than trusting the mode label. A zero deviation without valid signal evidence is not centered guidance. A false `verified` result calls for an instrument check, not repeated button presses.
4. Identify valid glide slope alive from the pointer moving toward center with valid signal evidence; it precedes active G/S capture. At that observed condition, execute gear DOWN/three-green, flaps 15, both engine-start switches CONT and speedbrake ARM checks, including the separate ARMED annunciator. Do not defer this action sequence while polling for capture. Observe flap limits and actual movement; lever percentages are not flap degrees. Read flight instruments between configuration changes and mark only items supported by the observed condition.
5. At G/S capture, select the directed landing flap setting and its verified VREF plus correction. Set the chart's missed-approach altitude only at the prescribed capture condition, then recheck that G/S remains active. Do not raise the selected altitude while continuing a negative V/S descent and expect it to protect against terrain.
6. For a documented autoland, continue promptly with `737-landing` to verify the second channel and armed landing modes while still high enough. If capture fails, guidance becomes invalid, or the aircraft is already above the glide slope close in, use `737-go-around`; do not repair final approach with heading, V/S or level-change mode substitutions.

## Decide after every read

- Monitor IAS/trend, altitude, height AGL, vertical speed, runway-relative position, valid deviations and active/armed modes together. Include `brakes-flaps-and-gear` readings: gear-handle DOWN, three safe gear indications, actual directed flaps and armed speedbrake. A flap command or task-list entry is not configuration evidence. Compare ground track with the inbound course; changing DME alone does not prove alignment or progress toward the threshold.
- Apply any stricter documented stabilized-approach gate. Otherwise, be stabilized by 1,000 ft above the airfield in IMC or 500 ft in VMC: correct lateral/vertical path, gear DOWN/three-green, directed landing flaps and checklist complete, the speed for those actual flaps plus correction, appropriate thrust, and normally no more than 1,000 ft/min descent. For a coupled ILS, require active VOR/LOC and G/S with deviations within approximately one dot unless the procedure is tighter; for autoland, also require the channels and armed modes in `737-landing`.
- If any gate requirement is failed or unverified, or subsequently lost, make the next control action a go-around under `737-go-around`; do not spend another read cycle trying to complete missing configuration below the gate. Late G/S capture does not reset the gate or make excessive sink safe. Armed G/S with active V/S is not an ILS descent. A low-altitude cross-track correction, increasing distance after passing the runway, full-scale deviations, or decreasing terrain clearance while off course requires discontinuation, not another observation loop.
- At minimums, continue only with the visual references or approved autoland conditions required by the procedure. Never use simulator height above terrain as a substitute for the chart's specified barometric or radio-altitude datum.
